# Careflow Clinic ERP — Phase 1 Outcomes (Historical Baseline)

> This file is the historical Phase 1 checklist preserved from the verified baseline. Its Phase 1-only mock-authentication, browser-persistence and “future backend” rules are superseded by the integrated Phase 3 implementation. See the project [README](../README.md) and [Phase 3 handoff](../PHASE3_HANDOFF.md) for current behavior. The interface/workflow requirements remain useful baseline context.

## [x] 1. Mock Login, role identification, and role-aware application shell

- A functional mock Login page is included and clearly labeled “Demo / mock authentication.” Demo user/role identification on Login leads to the correct role-specific dashboard and navigation; logout returns to Login. Do not implement real credentials, JWT, backend authentication, or real access control.
- The Phase 1 role set is exactly Clinic Administrator, Receptionist, Doctor, Pharmacist, Laboratory Technician, and Patient. Do not use Finance as a separate role. Patient is both a domain entity and a system role, with a patient-scoped experience for that synthetic patient’s own allowed information.
- Role-specific UI is demonstration behavior only, not a security boundary. Clinic Administrator has broad administration/reports/billing; Receptionist has registration, demographic/contact lookup, appointments, notifications and billing but not unrestricted Medical Records or clinical-history panels; Doctor sees own/assigned appointments, consultations and prescriptions; Pharmacist has dispensing and medicine/inventory/supplier/procurement workflows; Laboratory Technician has worklist/results; Patient has their own scoped dashboard, appointments, released results/records, prescriptions, notifications, profile and appropriate invoice information.
- The persistent grouped sidebar, top-level search, notifications, profile access, active-page indication, useful breadcrumbs/page headers and the responsive professional application shell are consistent across the authenticated demo experience. Patient UI does not expose staff registers or administration.

## [x] 2. Twenty-two ERP pages plus Login and operational dashboard

- Deliver the 22 distinct ERP pages Dashboard; Patients; Patient Profile; Register Patient; Medical Records; Appointments; Consultations; Prescriptions; Laboratory Worklist; Laboratory Results; Pharmacy; Inventory/Medicine Management; Suppliers; Procurement; Billing; Financial Reports; Operational Reports; User Administration; Audit Logs; System Administration; Notifications; and Profile, plus the additive Login page (23 distinct routes/pages total).
- Dashboard includes meaningful operational KPIs, appointments-by-day and monthly-revenue charts, laboratory/inventory status summaries, alerts and recent activity. Dashboard information and actions are role-appropriate; charts use synthetic data and have informational purpose.
- Related pages link through synthetic patient, appointment, record, prescription, lab order, medicine, supplier, procurement, invoice and audit identifiers. Do not create disconnected static screens.

## [x] 3. Patient, clinical, appointment, prescription, and laboratory workflows

- Use synthetic-only records. Patients are searchable/filterable/sortable/paginated and support create, view and edit plus safe archive/deactivation. Registration has labeled grouped fields, required-field indication and inline validation. Patient profile displays synthetic demographics/contact/emergency data and linked history; the Patient role can see only its own mock data.
- Appointments support date/status/provider filtering plus create, reschedule, cancel and complete actions with clear status feedback. Patient role sees only own appointments and limited self-service actions.
- Medical Records supports searchable encounter/record timelines; Consultations supports visit queues, linked visit details, mock note entry and status progression.
- Prescriptions support create/edit, patient/clinician linkage, status filters and dispensing handoff. Laboratory Worklist supports priority/status filtering, assignment and workflow updates; Laboratory Results supports validated result entry/review and linked status changes. Patient role sees only their own prescriptions and reviewed/released results/records.

## [x] 4. Pharmacy, medicine catalog CRUD, suppliers, and procurement

- Pharmacy supports prescription lookup, dispensing/fulfillment actions, status tracking and stock-aware cues. The domain workflow is prescription → dispensing → inventory deduction; no shopping cart or unrelated e-commerce behavior is included.
- Inventory/Medicine Management provides first-class medicine catalog create, view, edit, and archive/deactivate actions; search, filter, sort, stock quantity, minimum stock level, expiry date, supplier and stock status. Show computed in-stock, low-stock, out-of-stock and expired states. Support mock stock adjustment with an auditable event and low-stock/expiry cues. This is the Careflow equivalent of Product/Service Management, not an e-commerce catalog.
- Suppliers support create/view/edit, active/inactive status and links to medicines/procurement. Procurement supports purchase requests/orders with draft, submit, approve and receive transitions plus supplier/item selection. Receiving a mock order updates medicine stock and records audit activity.

## [x] 5. Billing and Financial/Operational Reports

- Billing supports invoice register/detail, patient linkage, create/edit and mock paid/part-paid/void state updates. Clinic Administrator and Receptionist handle billing; there is no separate Finance role.
- Financial Reports and Operational Reports use synthetic data and working date filters, useful chart/table output, and all three actions: Generate PDF, Export Excel as a usable `.xlsx`, and Print with a print-optimized layout. Both PDF and Excel outputs use the current report filters/data; actions provide clear success/error feedback.
- Centralized report API/service contracts expose report data and report-file methods so a future backend adapter can provide real report data/files without changing report pages. Phase 1 generates/exports entirely in the browser from mock data.

## [x] 6. Administration, audit, notifications, and profile

- User Administration supports user listing, create/edit/deactivate and assignment of only the six approved roles. System Administration provides frontend-only clinic settings/preferences. Audit Logs support free-text search and filters for actor, event date/time, area and action, and display synthetic actor/time/area/action details.
- Notifications are role-relevant, support read/unread updates and link to affected records. Profile supports current demo-user preferences and mock-session identity, logout and demo-session reset.

## [x] 7. Frontend-only architecture, data, and interaction quality

- All domain data operations go through a centralized typed API/service facade backed by mock adapters and synthetic seed fixtures. Methods return asynchronous results and remain replaceable with a backend adapter later. Demo edits and mock session may persist in browser storage, with a clear reset to the synthetic demo dataset/session. Do not build, enable or depend on backend/database services, real patient records, JWT, real auth, or external integrations in Phase 1.
- Relevant tables support search, practical filters, sorting, pagination, status badges, row actions and empty/loading states. Forms use consistent labels, validation and clear action feedback. Confirm consequential mock archive/void/cancel operations. Support responsive tablet/mobile use while prioritizing desktop density/readability.
- Use the required formal, credible hospital/clinic ERP visual system: white and blue dominate; #2563EB is primary, #16233B is dark navy, neutral grays are restrained, and green/red/amber are used sparingly for semantic statuses. No gradients, glassmorphism, neon, decorative AI-style graphics, futuristic effects, excessive rounded cards/pills, excessive shadows or animation, or generic SaaS landing-page styling. Prioritize functionality, usability, readability, consistency and professional healthcare UX.


## [x] 8. Appointment request, receptionist review, scheduling, conflict prevention, and notifications

- Extend the existing appointment model/system; do not create a separate appointment system. It must represent who requested the appointment and whether Patient or Doctor requested it, patient, doctor, requested date/time, confirmed date/time, current status, rejection/cancellation reason where applicable, created timestamp, and updated timestamp. Support the appointment states `Pending Approval`, `Approved`, `Scheduled`, `Checked In`, `In Consultation`, `Completed`, `Cancelled`, `Rejected`, `No Show`, and `Reschedule Requested`. Patient/Doctor requests start as `Pending Approval`; only Receptionist can move a pending request into a confirmed/scheduled appointment, which is `Scheduled`.
- Patient has a visible **Book Appointment** action and can choose Doctor, Department, Preferred date, Preferred time, Visit type, and Reason/notes. Patient ID is derived from the logged-in Patient mock session; Patient cannot choose another patient. Submission stays `Pending Approval`, does not create a confirmed appointment, appears in Receptionist’s queue, notifies Receptionist, and displays exactly: “Appointment request submitted. A receptionist will review the requested time and confirm your appointment.” Patient can view only own pending requests and confirmed appointments, request cancellation/change where appropriate, and cannot approve, confirm, book for another patient, or manually change appointment status.
- Doctor has a **Schedule Appointment** or equivalent action and can choose Patient, Preferred date/time, Visit type, and Reason/notes. Doctor ID is derived from the logged-in Doctor mock session. Submission stays `Pending Approval` and appears in Receptionist’s queue with a Receptionist notification. Doctor can view own appointments and relevant patient appointment information and request changes/cancellation, but cannot bypass Receptionist approval or directly confirm.
- Receptionist has an **Appointment Requests** queue showing Request ID, Patient, Doctor, Department, Requested date, Requested time, Requested by, Visit type, Status, and Created date/time. Each request has **Review**. Review shows the patient’s existing appointments around the requested date/time and the doctor’s existing appointments around that date/time so Receptionist can determine availability for both.
- Receptionist can **Approve & Schedule**, **Suggest Another Time**, **Reject**, and **Cancel** where appropriate. Approval is allowed only when both patient and doctor are available; approval sets status to `Scheduled` and makes the appointment confirmed. Reschedule alternatives must be available for both patient and doctor. A suggested time stays pending until the relevant Patient/Doctor accepts the proposal; at minimum, a changed/proposed time generates a notification. Rejection/cancellation reasons are stored where appropriate.
- Prevent Receptionist approval if the doctor already has an appointment in that slot, the patient already has an appointment in that slot, the doctor is marked unavailable, or the patient is marked unavailable. Show a clear conflict reason, for example: “Cannot schedule this appointment. Dr. Sharma already has an appointment at 10:30 AM.” Display patient and doctor availability around the requested slot; the example slot view includes 10:00, 10:30, 11:00, and 11:30, with existing appointments marked and available slots clear. The centralized API must repeat conflict validation on approval, not rely only on disabled UI.
- The Receptionist schedule/register is a professional compact table view, not a heavyweight calendar; it shows doctor schedules, patient schedules, existing appointments, pending requests, available slots, conflicting slots, and synthetic unavailability blocks. Receptionist can review all requests, check patient/doctor availability, approve, reject, reschedule, cancel, and view schedules. Clinic Administrator retains existing administrative permissions; only Receptionist approves/schedules pending requests.
- Use the existing centralized API facade (`api.create()`, `api.update()`, `api.list()`, etc.); add typed facade methods if needed. Do not add direct `fetch` calls, backend/database, real authentication, or change the six roles. Patient/Doctor/Receptionist create, review, and mutate appointment states only through mock API methods and synthetic browser data.
- Send centralized identity/role-targeted notifications: Patient receives appointment request submitted, approved/confirmed, rejected, time changed, and cancelled notifications; Doctor receives request submitted/received, approved, time changed, and cancelled notifications; Receptionist receives Patient appointment request, Doctor appointment request, reschedule request, and cancellation request notifications. Patient notification lists must remain scoped to the logged-in Patient; Doctor notifications must target the relevant Doctor. Notification links lead to the related appointment workflow.
- Patient dashboard separates **Pending Requests** from **Confirmed Appointments** and shows upcoming items. Doctor dashboard shows appointment requests/relevant appointments, today’s schedule, and upcoming appointments. Receptionist dashboard shows Appointment Requests, Pending Approval count, today’s confirmed appointments, and today’s doctor availability/conflicts. Keep other roles and unrelated pages/workflows unchanged.
- Demonstrate the exact user acceptance scenarios with synthetic data: (A) Patient submits a Doctor/date/time request, status is `Pending Approval`, and Receptionist sees it; (B) Receptionist reviews both schedules, approves when available, status becomes `Scheduled`, and Patient + Doctor receive notifications; (C) an occupied doctor slot visibly conflicts and cannot be approved; (D) Doctor creates a Patient appointment request as `Pending Approval` and Receptionist is notified; (E) Receptionist proposes another available time and the affected user receives a notification; (F) Patient sees only their own appointments.
